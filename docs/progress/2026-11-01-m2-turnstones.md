# B3 wave 2: the turnstones (m2-turnstones)

Status: Done

A small winter flock comes to wrack you leave lying on the strandline. Before their home exists, a few birds visit in autumn or winter and run the strand for an hour, and the next morning a note hints at what would keep them. Once wrack has lain 10 m long for 3 days, a flock of 4 to 12 settles (one bird for every 2.5 m of wrack). If you gather the wrack up, they grow uneasy and leave after 3 days. From mid-spring to the start of autumn they are away north, and their journal page says so. The hourly tick runs them on the minnows' arc with the wrack as their pool (`src/world/creatures/flock.js`, ready for the purple sandpipers), seeded and safe through catch-up. Nothing changes on an island without a home, so the hash stays C43ABA37.

On the strand each bird is R1's turnstone on the small-bird rig. They run to wrack, flip it with their bills, peck, preen and look about, and they roost tight on the highest dry spot at high tide or at night. An interference check stops any two bodies overlapping, whichever way they face. Their plumage turns with the year (Matt, November 1, 2026): chestnut-and-black as they come back in early autumn, winter brown by mid-autumn, and back to chestnut before they leave, each bird turning at its own moment. Only the walker startles them, never the camera. At Wary they flush low over the water, chattering, and land 18 to 30 m along the strand; past Wary they run aside. Trust comes from watching only and stops at Curious. At 0.5 comes their group moment: they forage around your boots. They take no treats, have no name and give no gift. Their voice is made in code: a flush chatter, a feeding "tuk" and twitters at the roost. They have a full journal page with a sketch, hints, field notes and web links, plus a sketchbook entry, a drawn icon, a morning hint and test links (`?turnstones`, `&trust=`, `?shape=wrack&visit=turnstones`).
