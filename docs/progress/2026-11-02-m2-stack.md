# F19, the light on the stack (claude/m2-stack-3a2727)

Status: Done

A tall terraced sea stack now stands offshore on every island, on the open-sea side away from the meadow islet: a craggy core about 11.5 m high (the ground's ceiling is 12 m), four uneven ledges stepping down one side with thrift on them, a foot apron above every tide and a cleft in its landward face where the cave mouth is. Round it a 25 m no-build ring: the brushes leave its cells alone (a fixed guard under the Steps' own), its edge shows dashed like the camp pad's, and on an older island land raised inside it sinks back once. Go down never sets you on the stack: aimed there, you come down on the shore (or on the causeway, once it stands). On two or three seeded evenings of its first week a warm light shows at its foot after dusk, with moths round it. At the first low water of spring day 7 time holds, the view glides out, and the causeway rises stone by stone from the shore to the stack's foot over about 12 seconds; its line goes in the journal and it stands above every tide from then on. An island past day 7 gets the stack on its next load, the lights over the next evenings and the road seven days later.

The mouth room is a zone like the spring's cave, with its own light: daylight down the cleft by the way in, and the old lantern lying cold on the floor. Light it and hang it by the arch and it becomes a real flickering point light, with soft shadows baked into the rock, ambient occlusion in the hollows, a warm bounce round it, a glow in the air, glints in wet stone and its horn panes alight. Beside the lantern lies the lamplighter's first note (a keeper page). Seeing the arch adds the driftwood staff (a stick and rush cord) to the workbench; the arch stays closed until the Hollow. The rubble off the other side is dug a stretch a day with the shovel.
