# F19, the light on the stack (claude/m2-stack-3a2727)

Status: Done

A tall terraced sea stack now stands offshore on every island, on the open-sea side away from the meadow islet: a craggy core about 11.5 m high (the ground's ceiling is 12 m), four uneven ledges stepping down one side with thrift on them, a foot apron above every tide, and on it Matt's cave mouth model: a rock outcrop with an arched opening toward the island and a roofed chamber that reads dark in any weather (decimated to 14,000 triangles, ambient occlusion baked, shadow cast). Round it a 25 m no-build ring: the brushes leave its cells alone (a fixed guard under the Steps' own), its edge shows dashed like the camp pad's, and on an older island land raised inside it sinks back once. Go down never sets you on the stack: aimed there, you come down on the shore (or on the causeway, once it stands). On two or three seeded evenings of its first week a warm light shows at its foot after dusk, with moths round it. At the first low water of spring day 7 time holds, the view glides out, and the causeway rises stone by stone from the shore to the stack's foot over about 12 seconds; its line goes in the journal and it stands above every tide from then on. An island past day 7 gets the stack on its next load, the lights over the next evenings and the road seven days later.

The mouth room is a zone like the spring's cave, about 13 m across, with its own light: daylight from above the way in, a rock pool of murky still water, and the old lantern lying cold on the floor. Light it, carry it to its iron hook by the arch and hang it and it becomes a real flickering point light, with soft shadows baked into the rock, ambient occlusion in the hollows, a warm bounce round it, a glow in the air, glints in wet stone and its horn panes alight. Beside the lantern lies the lamplighter's first note (a keeper page). The arch's own stone is blackened with old soot and scored with signs; seeing it adds the driftwood staff (a stick and rush cord) to the workbench; the arch stays closed until the Hollow. The rubble off the other side, stacked from the floor up, is dug a stretch a day with the shovel.
