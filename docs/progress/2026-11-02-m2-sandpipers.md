# B3 wave 3: the purple sandpipers (m2-sandpipers)

Status: Done

A small, tame winter flock comes to a Terrace sea wall you build in the splash zone, at least 8 m long. The island's own rocky point never counts. Before the wall exists, a few birds visit a shore in autumn or winter and creep along the water's edge for an hour, and the next morning a note hints at what would keep them. Once the wall stands, a flock of 3 to 10 settles, one bird for every 1.6 m of wall, and only one flock per island (Matt, November 1, 2026). Take the wall down and they grow uneasy and leave after 3 days. They are away north from early spring to mid-autumn, later to come and later to leave than the turnstones, and their journal page says so. The hourly tick runs them on the turnstones' flock arc with the wall as their pool, seeded and safe through catch-up. Nothing changes on an island without a wall.

Each bird is made in code from the turnstone's recipe on the same small-bird rig: a longer bill with a slight droop, shorter yellow-orange legs, a slate-grey back with a purple cast and a pale belly, at the turnstone's budget (3,000 triangles, 1,196 for a flock bird). They share the turnstones' live code, which gained three moves from the recipe: a low creep over the wet rock, a bob, and a tucked roost on the wall's top at high tide, heads turned back into their feathers. Only the walker startles them, and they flush low and tight over the water to land further along the wall. Trust comes from watching only and stops at Curious; at 0.5 they creep around your boots. They take no treats, have no name and bring no gift, and their page says so. They have a voice made in code (a soft kwit as they flush, a wit while feeding, twitters at the roost), a full journal page, web links, a sketchbook entry, a drawn icon, a morning hint and test links (`?shape=wall&sandpipers`, `&trust=`, `?shape=wall&visit=sandpipers`).
