# Finds on the shore

Small shore lives and shells to find by tapping (docs/future/11). Where they live comes from how you shape the coast. Nothing about them is saved; the journal keeps what you have found under **Living things > Small lives**, which lists only what you have found.

| Find | Where | What it does | Made from |
|---|---|---|---|
| Beadlet anemone | Rock pools (a hollow of rock that reaches the tide band) | Open under water; closes to a dome at low tide or when you come close | "Low-Poly Anemone" by Ben Lebreux, CC-BY-4.0 |
| Periwinkle | On the wrack | Groups creep a little each tide | CC0 shell scan, cut to 900 triangles |
| Lugworm casts | Wet sand and mud the tide has left | Laid fresh after each tide, with a burrow dip beside each | Drawn into the sand by the terrain shader, no mesh |
| Hermit crab | Rock pools | Walks about the pool floor under water (its own rig and walk); stops when you come close or the pool drains | CC0 model, 1,489 triangles, 28 bones |
| Young edible crab | In the wrack and the pools | Runs sideways away from you, then settles back | "Crab 3d Model Free" by iRahulRajput, CC-BY-4.0 |
| Shells | Along each high tide's strandline | Each tide leaves new ones; rare ones only on the biggest tides near new and full moon. One Shells page keeps each kind found | Turret, fossil cockle, murex and nautilus, each CC-BY-4.0 (credits below); the periwinkle shell reuses the scan |

Every find is drawn at least 1.5 times real size so it can be found (anemones and periwinkles 2 times). Credits for every CC-BY model are in README.md and `assets/models/finds/CREDITS.md`.

Also in this branch: strandline litter no longer draws under standing water (it rang a dug pool at every tide level and the water bent it into streaks), and every find's journal sketch is redrawn around its model's own traced shape.

## Click-test

1. Paint rock along the shore in the tide band and lower a hollow in it. When the tide has filled it, red-pink anemones sit open in the pool, and often a hermit crab in an orange shell. No streaky litter lines show under the pool's water.
2. Zoom right in on an anemone: it closes to a dome. Pull back: it opens again. At low tide it stays closed.
3. Watch the hermit crab from a little way off: it walks slowly about the pool, its legs moving, smoothly. Zoom close: it stops.
4. Look along the weed on the rocks: small periwinkles in groups, now and then a small red crab. Zoom in near a crab: it runs sideways away, then settles.
5. At low tide, look along the wet sand: coils of sand, each with a small dip beside it. After the next tide they are in new places.
6. Walk the strandline (the line of litter at the top of the beach): small shells lie in it, mostly periwinkle and turret shells. Tap one: the card names it and says how rare. Open the Shells page: it lists the ones you found and pencil hints for the rest.
7. Tap any find: its page has a sketch that looks like the model. Compare them all on tools/finds-sketches.html.
8. On your phone, check that the Small lives list and the find pages fit with no sideways scroll.
9. Open tools/finds-models.html: every model at true scale; the hermit crab walks; drag to turn them.
